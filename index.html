Windstorm Impact, Science, and Engineering

The WISE Research Group, led by Aly Mousaad Aly, Ph.D., P.E., at Louisiana State University

We study how wind and other flows load structures, from buildings, bridges, and solar arrays to wind turbines and airfoils, and we develop the testing methods, simulations, and control systems that let engineers design them to survive.

Why it matters

Hurricanes are the costliest weather disasters in the United States: of the billion-dollar disasters NOAA has recorded since 1980, tropical cyclones account for more than half of all losses (NOAA NCEI, 2025). The group’s work turns that risk into safer design, from the test floor to the classroom.

Public safety

Safer buildings and cladding

Large-scale tests found peak pressures 25–300% higher than small-scale tests, the pressures that break windows and roofs in storms.

Resilient energy

Solar power that survives storms

Rooftop solar arrays reduced roof wind forces by 45–63%, and machine learning predicts wind loads on solar trackers up to 10,000 times faster than conventional CFD.

Transportation

Bridges protected from truck strikes

A sandwich cushion cut a struck girder’s deflection by 89% in simulations, and a patented sensor network warns drivers of overheight trucks before impact.

Research infrastructure

A large-scale test capability

The LSU WISE open-jet facility reproduces complete atmospheric boundary-layer turbulence at Reynolds numbers up to 2.3 × 106, depending on the test model, and can test models to failure.

Where we test

Open-jet instrumentation includes two Cobra probes for three-component velocity, five accelerometers, two load cells, and a 256-channel pressure measuring system, with a shake table, four MR dampers, and a laser displacement sensor for smart-structure studies.

What we study

Four core lines of work, from measuring wind loads at large scale to predicting them with machine learning and controlling how structures respond. The illustrations are schematic; measured results and their sources are in the text below each one.

Large-scale open-jet testing

The LSU WISE open-jet facility reproduces complete atmospheric boundary-layer turbulence at Reynolds numbers up to 2.3 × 106, depending on the test model, with integral length scales about ten times those of conventional small-scale testing.

Measured surface pressures agree closely with full-scale field data and run 25–300% higher than small-scale tests (Physics of Fluids, 2024).

Aerodynamics, CFD, and machine learning

Coupling machine learning with computational fluid dynamics to predict wind loads at a fraction of the cost, with extensions to turbulence modeling for RANS simulations and to the unsteady aerodynamics of pitching and plunging airfoils (AIAA Journal, 2026).

Wind loads on solar panels predicted up to 10,000 times faster than conventional CFD, with accuracy preserved (Sustainable Cities and Society, 2023).

Structural dynamics, smart structures, and control

Smart dampers and tuned pendulum pounding mass dampers that reduce vibration in tall buildings and wind turbines under wind and earthquake loads, verified in experiments (Soil Dynamics and Earthquake Engineering, 2023).

A physics-based theory of semiactive control gains for smart dampers in multi-degree-of-freedom structures (ASCE Journal of Structural Engineering, 2019).

Wind-resilient solar and agrivoltaics

Wind loads and aeroelastic stability of rooftop and ground-mounted solar arrays, solar trackers, and agrivoltaic systems that share land between crops and energy.

Rooftop PV arrays reduced total wind-induced roof forces by 45–63% (Sustainable and Resilient Infrastructure, 2023).

AA

Aly Mousaad Aly, Ph.D., P.E.

Associate Professor, Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Louisiana State University, and Director, WISE Research Group

Trained as a mechanical engineer (B.Sc. and M.Sc., Alexandria University; Ph.D., Politecnico di Milano), Dr. Aly works where fluid mechanics meets structural dynamics. Before joining LSU in 2013, he was a postdoctoral researcher at Florida International University’s International Hurricane Research Center, an assistant professor of mechanical engineering at Alexandria University, and a research fellow at Western University’s Boundary Layer Wind Tunnel Laboratory. He founded and directs the WISE Research Group, established the LSU WISE open-jet facility, and is a licensed Professional Engineer in Louisiana.

What support would make possible

These directions build directly on the group’s results and are ready to grow with agency and industry support.

Energy

Solar and agrivoltaic systems in hurricane winds

Extend large-scale tests and machine-learning CFD from rooftop panels and trackers to full arrays and agrivoltaic farms, including torsional galloping of trackers.

Design tools

Faster, validated wind loads

Pair open-jet measurements with machine-learning CFD so engineers can predict loads on buildings and cladding in minutes rather than days.

Structures

Calmer structures in wind and earthquakes

Carry the pendulum pounding damper and smart-damper research from models and shake tables to tall timber buildings and wind turbines.

Transportation

Safer bridges and highways

Develop and field-test overheight-vehicle detection and protection, and vibration control for traffic signal structures.

Workforce

Students at the bench

Support graduate and undergraduate researchers who build, test, simulate, and publish: the engineers these problems need.
