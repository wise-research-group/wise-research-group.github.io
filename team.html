The people who test the wind.

The Windstorm Impact, Science, and Engineering (WISE) Research Group, led by Dr. Aly Mousaad Aly at Louisiana State University, studies how extreme wind loads buildings, bridges, solar arrays, and wind turbines, and how to design them to survive. This page is the team: who is here now, who graduated, and where they went.

The group has graduated 7 Ph.D. students and 19 M.S. students, mentored 19 undergraduate researchers and 2 postdoctoral researchers, and co-authored 38 journal articles with graduate students and 7 with students who started in the group as undergraduates.

AA

Aly Mousaad Aly, Ph.D., P.E.

Associate Professor, Louisiana State University, and Director, WISE Research Group

Dr. Aly founded and directs the WISE Research Group and oversees the LSU WISE open-jet testing facility, which reproduces atmospheric boundary-layer turbulence at high Reynolds numbers for large-scale aerodynamic testing. His group works at the intersection of wind engineering, structural dynamics and control, and machine-learning-accelerated computational fluid dynamics. He is a member of ASCE and ASME and a licensed Professional Engineer in Louisiana.

Hurricane wind generation in the open-jet simulator, 2013–2016; LSU President's Future Leaders in Research; co-author on a journal article

My research at LSU focuses on the effects of wind loads on roofs and roof-mounted solar panels. I am studying why solar panels fail when subjected to loads often less than the advertised load capacity. In performing this research, I learned about the fluid mechanics and dynamics of wind, and ran computational fluid dynamics simulations in OpenFOAM to visualize flow fields and measure pressure and velocity profiles. I developed many skills that I believe will help me for the rest of my life: diligence, self-discipline, time management, communication, and the ability to learn new information quickly. These allowed me to publish a journal article with my research mentor, maintain a 4.2 GPA, and earn several scholarships.

Raymond Poche, undergraduate researcher
